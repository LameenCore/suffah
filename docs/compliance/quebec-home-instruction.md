# Quebec home-instruction: the requirements Suffa's compliance record is meant to evidence

_Date: 2026-09-05 · Task T40 · **Not legal advice.** This is a working summary for
the team, compiled from the Quebec government's own guidance pages and the AQED
(Association québécoise pour l'éducation à domicile). Every family's obligation is
set by the current statute and regulation, not by this file or by the product.
See the lawyer-review checklist at the bottom — nothing here should be relied on
until it is confirmed._

## The legal framework

- **Education Act (Loi sur l'instruction publique, "LIP")** — homeschooling
  ("l'enseignement à la maison" / "instruction in the family home") is a
  recognised way to satisfy the obligation to attend school. The 2017 amendments
  in **Bill 144 (*An Act to amend the Education Act and other legislative
  provisions concerning mainly free educational services and compulsory school
  attendance*)** added the oversight regime.
- **Regulation respecting instruction in the family home** ("Règlement sur
  l'enseignement à la maison", in force 2018–2019) — sets the notice, learning
  project, status report, two progress reports, evaluation, ministerial-exam and
  monitoring-meeting obligations, and who they are filed with: the **Direction de
  l'enseignement à la maison (DEM)** at the ministère de l'Éducation.
- Section numbers are **deliberately not cited here** — they must be pulled from
  the current consolidated text (LégisQuébec) at review time, because the
  regulation has been amended since 2019.

## The obligations (what a family must do each year)

| # | Obligation | What it is | Timing (as published) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Notice of intent** | Annual notice to the DEM (and the school service centre) that the child will be home-instructed | "no later than **July 1**", or within **10 days** of the child's last day of school if withdrawing mid-year |
| 2 | **Learning project** ("projet d'apprentissage") | The plan: educational approach, programs of study + teaching activities, other knowledge/skills, educational resources, **time-allocation plan**, contributing organisations, **evaluation methods**, and the child's last institutional grade level | Submit the required information and **start implementing no later than September 30**; within **30 days** if starting mid-year |
| 3 | **Status report** ("état de situation") | A snapshot of the learning activities actually carried out and the approximate time allocated to them | Once in the year (exact date set by the regulation / the DEM resource person) |
| 4 | **Two progress reports** ("bilans de progression") | The parent describes the child's progress **against the expected outcomes of the programs of study** | **Mid-project bilan** ("bilan de mi-parcours"): **3–5 months after implementation begins** (e.g. Dec–Feb for a September start). **End-of-project bilan** ("bilan de fin de projet"): end of the year |
| 5 | **Annual evaluation** | The child must be evaluated **at least once** during the year, by one of four accepted modes (below) | Result recorded on the mid-project and/or end-of-project bilan |
| 6 | **Ministerial examinations** | "All students and homeschooled children take examinations set by the Minister" — the same provincial exams as schooled students, in the subjects/years they apply | Per the ministry's exam calendar |
| 7 | **Monitoring meeting** ("rencontre de suivi") | The parent and child meet the DEM to discuss the experience, the implementation of the learning project, and the child's progress | Once in the year, scheduled by the DEM |

### The four accepted evaluation modes (obligation 5)

1. Evaluation by the **school service centre / school board** (centre de services scolaire).
2. Evaluation by a **private educational institution** holding a permit.
3. Evaluation by a **holder of a Quebec teaching licence** — a written evaluation prepared by a certified teacher.
4. **Portfolio** — the parent analyses the child's activities and work and submits the portfolio to the DEM (attached to the **end-of-project bilan**, not the mid-project one).

Modes 1–3 are done by a third party ("évaluateur"); mode 4 is done by the parent.

## How Suffa's record maps to the obligations

Suffa **organises the evidence**; it does not replace the parent's filings or the
ministerial exams. The mapping:

| Suffa produces | Evidences / supports | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Per-course **pathway + progress** (node position, checkpoints passed) | Obligation 3 (status report): "learning activities carried out" and time; Obligation 4 (progress reports): activity record | The parent still writes the narrative bilan; Suffa gives them the activity log to write it from |
| **Unit-assessment results** (objective, per unit) | Obligation 4: progress "in relation to the expected outcomes of the programs of study" — for the courses that map to a Quebec program (Math) | Seerah / AI-literacy are **not** Quebec programs — see checklist |
| **Term-exam results** (timed, per course) | Obligation 4 evidence of mastery; a **practice/parallel** to Obligation 6 (ministerial exams) | **Does not substitute** for the ministerial exam — flagged in-product and in the checklist |
| **Living compliance status** ("on track / watch / gap forming") per course + overall | An early-warning tool for the parent and the masjid — **not a regulatory artifact** | Framed in the UI as illustrative; thresholds are the product's, not the ministry's |
| **Compliance report snapshot** (assembled + printable, per student per term) | A packaging of the above the parent can bring to the monitoring meeting (Obligation 7) or give an evaluator (Obligation 5, modes 1–3) | It is an **input** to those processes, not the filing itself |
| **Objective-only grading** (MCQ / short numeric) | Reliability of the assessment record | The regulation's progress reports expect judgement against program outcomes, which includes competencies Suffa does not grade — see checklist |

**In-product framing (already implemented, T16):** every screen that states an
exemption threshold or an evaluation format carries a "verify with current
regulation" note, and the compliance report says its status thresholds are
illustrative.

## Checklist for a Quebec education lawyer / the DEM — confirm before any family relies on this

1. **The ≤4-student pod cap.** The product enforces "fewer than 5 students per
   instructor" and attributes it to Quebec's home-instruction exemption. Confirm
   the actual basis: is it the point at which a group teaching activity becomes a
   "school" requiring a permit under the *Act respecting private education* / the
   Education Act, or something else? Get the citation and the exact count and
   conditions (per instructor? per site? per session?).
2. **Learning-project sufficiency.** Does an AI-delivered curriculum + a masjid
   volunteer for enrichment satisfy the "educational approach", "teaching
   activities", and "educational resources" elements? Must the parent still be
   named as the person responsible, and to what degree must they be the one
   instructing?
3. **Program mapping.** Math is mapped to a Quebec program. Confirm the specific
   *Programme de formation de l'école québécoise* competencies and the Secondary 1
   progression the demo unit claims to cover. Seerah and AI-literacy have no
   Quebec program — confirm how "other knowledge and skills" in the learning
   project is meant to be reported and evaluated for those.
4. **Evaluation modes.** For mode 3 (certified teacher) and mode 4 (portfolio):
   what must the portfolio contain, and can Suffa's persisted lesson/checkpoint/
   assessment artifacts + the printable report constitute a compliant portfolio,
   or only feed one the parent assembles?
5. **Ministerial exams.** Confirm which years/subjects in the 10–13 band trigger a
   ministerial exam, how a homeschooled child registers, and that the product's
   copy never implies the term exam substitutes for it.
6. **Objective-only grading.** The progress reports expect judgement against
   program *competencies*, some of which are not multiple-choice gradeable. Is an
   objective-only assessment record acceptable as the parent's supporting
   evidence, with the parent supplying the competency judgement?
7. **Filing responsibility and data.** Confirm the parent (not the masjid, not
   Suffa) remains the filer for all seven obligations, and that sharing a child's
   record with the masjid admin / a third-party evaluator through the product is
   consistent with Law 25 and the ministry's expectations (ties to T36/T37).
8. **Currency.** Re-pull the consolidated LIP sections and the *Règlement sur
   l'enseignement à la maison* from LégisQuébec at review time and diff against
   this summary — it was compiled from guidance pages, not the statute.
