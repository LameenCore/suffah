# What is Suffa?

_Task T70. The 20-second version, no jargon._

## The problem

Some Muslim families in Quebec teach their kids at home instead of sending them
to school. To make it work, a few families share a room at the masjid and take
turns having a volunteer teach the group — a "pod." It's cheaper than private
school and the kids aren't isolated. But it keeps breaking: the volunteer who
teaches Grade 6 math moves away, and the teaching stops until someone else can
step in. On top of that, Quebec law requires the family to show, on a schedule,
that the child is actually learning — and right now families assemble that
proof by hand.

## What Suffa does

Suffa lets the **software teach the lessons** — reading material, practice,
quizzes, exams — so instruction never stops when a volunteer leaves. The
volunteers stay, but now they do the in-person part: discussion, projects,
mentorship. When one leaves, the incoming volunteer gets a short written
briefing on how the group has been learning, so nothing is lost. And every
quiz and exam result automatically builds the progress record the family needs
for Quebec — as a live "on track / needs attention" view, not a scramble at
term-end.

## Who does what

```
Family        → watches their child's progress (read-only, no work)
Student       → learns at their own pace in the playground
Volunteer     → runs live enrichment sessions; gets a briefing on handoff
Masjid admin  → runs the pods, the volunteers, and the compliance records
```

## How it's paid for

Not tuition. The masjid raises a permanent endowment (a *waqf*) once; only the
interest it earns is spent, never the principal. Families pay a small flat fee
that's the same for everyone, and community donations (*sadaqah*) cover that fee
for families who can't pay it. Because the software teaches the lessons once and
reuses them, adding another child costs almost nothing — so it works from the
very first family, without needing to grow big first.
